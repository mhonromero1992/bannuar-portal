# Voice-over script: Bannuar showcase video

For `bannuar-showcase.mp4` (9:25). Each line starts at its timecode and is written to fit the time on
screen at a calm pace, about 130 words a minute, with room to breathe. The captions on screen say the
same things in fewer words, so the narration adds to them rather than reads them out.

- **Voice:** warm, unhurried, confident. A friendly office manager, not a radio advert.
- **Pace:** if a line runs long, slow down the next screen rather than speed up. Every line fits its slot.
- **Pronunciation:** Bannuar = *ba-NOO-ar*. LESP = say the letters, *L-E-S-P*. DOLE = *dole*.
  ₱ = "pesos". "201 file" = *two-oh-one file*.
- **Recording:** mix the voice about 10 dB above the music (the music sits at about −17 dBFS RMS).

Word count: about 900 words.

---

## Opening

**0:01**: Bannuar Security Agency. One system for the office, for its clients, and for every guard on duty.

**0:10**: *(chapter card, pause)* First, the website.

## 1. The website (0:09 – 1:10)

**0:15**: This is Bannuar on the web: a town kept safe through the night, until sunrise.

**0:23**: Companies see the cover they can get: day, night, or round the clock.

**0:31**: Posts that HR opens in the portal show here. Applicants pick one and apply.

**0:39**: The application and resume go straight to HR, ready under Recruitment.

**0:46**: A company that needs guards asks for a proposal: where, how many, which hours.

**0:53**: It goes into Bannuar's own system. The office calls back with a proposal.

**1:00**: And anyone can check a certificate of employment. Type the code, and the site says if it's genuine.

## 2. The HR portal (1:10 – 4:31)

**1:11**: *(chapter card, pause)* Next, the HR portal, where the office works.

**1:16**: Staff join by invitation, and each role sees only its own part.

**1:22**: The dashboard starts with what's waiting: leave, certificates, payroll concerns and new applications.

**1:30**: And the day at a glance: guards on the roll, time in and out, expiring papers.

**1:38**: Every guard has a 201 file. Search, or filter by status and post.

**1:45**: One page holds everything about a guard, from deployment to payslips.

**1:52**: When a guard sends a license photo, Claude reads the number, name and expiry. HR still verifies it.

**2:01**: Each log has a photo, the server's time and the distance from the post. Far ones are flagged.

**2:10**: Open a log to see the photo and where the phone was.

**2:17**: The commander endorses leave, HR approves it, and the guard is told.

**2:24**: Certificate requests show their due date, three days as DOLE requires, and late ones stand out.

**2:33**: Each certificate carries Bannuar's badge, the signatory, and a code with a QR link, so anyone can check it.

**2:44**: Payroll works the way each client pays. Every client has its own pay rule, written as simple formulas.

**2:53**: Like the night differential: the last guard of the day earns ten percent extra for eight hours.

**3:01**: Each post has its own hourly rate, starting on a date, so past months never change.

**3:09**: Duties come from time in and out. Clean ones count; payroll decides the rest.

**3:17**: Clients confirm their duties within two days. Pay is never held up waiting.

**3:25**: Then pay is worked out for every guard: basic, overtime and night differential.

**3:34**: Even the thirteenth month follows each post. Dante's three posts add up to twenty-one thousand, three hundred twenty pesos.

**3:46**: Upload the Code of Discipline as a PDF. Claude turns it into rules for HR to approve.

**3:54**: Every case follows due process: a notice, time to explain, a hearing if asked, then a written decision.

**4:03**: Announcements go to everyone, an area, or chosen posts, and HR sees who acknowledged.

**4:10**: Applicants from the website are hired into a 201 file in one step.

**4:17**: HR, payroll, operations and management each get their own access.

**4:24**: And every change is on record, down to who opened whose government numbers.

## 3. The client portal (4:31 – 5:36)

**4:32**: *(chapter card, pause)* Then, the client portal.

**4:38**: Operations gives each client their own login. They see their own posts, and nothing else.

**4:47**: On a tablet, the client sees who is on duty right now, and since when.

**4:56**: Time in and out, day by day: who came, who left, and if they were at the post.

**5:04**: At each cutoff, the client confirms the duties served,

**5:12**: or dispute one. Silence for two days counts as confirmed. Bannuar checks every dispute.

**5:19**: Clients can report an incident and follow Bannuar's response.

**5:27**: And ask for more guards, a replacement, or a new schedule.

## 4. The employee app (5:36 – 9:13)

**5:37**: *(chapter card, pause)* Finally, the app on every guard's phone.

**5:43**: Guards sign in with their employee number. First time? They activate in the app.

**5:50**: Home shows today: time in, the post, notices from HR and the latest payslip.

**5:58**: To time in, the phone finds its place.

**6:02**: Bea is at the post, forty-two meters from the main gate.

**6:09**: Then a photo, taken right now.

**6:15**: Logged at 5:52, by the server's clock, not the phone's.

**6:21**: That one time in shows up in three places at once: Bea's phone, HR's attendance list, and the client's tablet.

**6:34**: Pay stays private. Amounts are hidden until the guard taps,

**6:41**: and screenshots are blocked on payslips.

**6:47**: Every peso is explained: basic pay, overtime, night differential, and each deduction.

**6:55**: Leave credits are always in view.

**7:02**: Filing leave takes a minute: the kind, the dates, and a reason.

**7:09**: And the guard always knows where a request stands.

**7:16**: Need a certificate for a loan? Request it from the phone.

**7:23**: It arrives in the app, with a code anyone can check.

**7:31**: Announcements are read and acknowledged with one tap.

**7:38**: When a license is about to expire, the guard uploads the new one,

**7:45**: and Claude starts reading it right away,

**7:49**: checking the number, the expiry and the name against the guard's record.

**7:57**: Notices from HR come with due process: the rule, what happened, and the deadline to answer.

**8:05**: Payslips, announcements, expiring licenses: nothing is missed.

**8:13**: And at night, the app goes dark, easy on the eyes on the night shift.

**8:22**: Detachment commanders get more.

**8:29**: Their post, their guards, and the leave waiting for them.

**8:36**: They endorse leave with a reliever, so the post is always covered.

**8:44**: Next: Carlo came to work without his phone.

**8:50**: The commander logs his time in for him, with Carlo's photo.

**8:57**: And Carlo is told in his own app.

**9:04**: Incidents at the post go straight to HR, with the time and the guard involved.

## Closing

**9:14**: Bannuar. The office, the clients and the guards, on one system.

**9:19**: *(silence; let the music finish)*
